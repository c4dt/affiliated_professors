Rüdiger Fahlenbrach is a Full Professor of Finance at EPFL and Director of the College of Management, specializing in empirical corporate finance with a focus on corporate governance, entrepreneurship, startups/unicorns, venture capital, and bank regulation.

### 2026-09-XX

- **New forthcoming publication:** "Direct democracy, corporate political strategy, and firm value" (with Alexei Ovtchinnikov and Philip Valta) accepted and forthcoming in *Management Science* — this paper was previously tracked under the SSRN title "Corporate Contributions to Ballot Measure Campaigns" and is now confirmed as forthcoming in a top journal.
- **Three new 2026 working papers** (not previously recorded) listed on the lab website:
  - "The global rise of index-based ownership of firms" (with Bo Becker and Ehsan Mahdikhani, 2026) — builds a 5-variable model identifying index-based investors globally, documenting growth from 3% to 16.9% of global market cap (2004–2023).
  - "Politics, regulation, and bank payouts" (with Minsu Ko and René Stulz, 2026) — updated/expanded version of the NBER WP 32770; now includes analysis of Trump election effects on large bank repurchases and returns.
  - "Leading by example: Can one universal shareholder's voting pre-disclosure influence voting outcomes?" (with Nicolas Rudolf and Alexis Wegerich, 2026) — studies how a large shareholder's pre-disclosed voting decisions shift other shareholders' votes by ~2.7 percentage points.

### 2026-07-10

- **New NBER Working Paper (May 2026):** "Organization Capital, Large Startups, and the Dearth of IPOs" (with Leandro Sanz and René M. Stulz) — proposes and tests a causal explanation for why organization-capital-intensive startups (relying on economies of scale and network effects via digital technologies) prefer to remain large private firms rather than IPO, using LinkedIn workforce data and the 2016 Defend Trade Secrets Act as a legal shock.
- **Working Paper (2024):** "Bank Payout Policy, Regulation, and Politics" released as NBER WP 32770 — examines post-crisis regulatory influences on bank dividend and buyback decisions.
